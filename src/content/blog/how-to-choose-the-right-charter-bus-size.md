Booking group transportation starts with one question: how many people are actually going? Get
that number wrong and you either pay for empty seats or end up short on seats and scrambling for a
second vehicle. Here's how to think through vehicle size before you book.

## Start With a Firm Headcount

Get a confirmed count, not an estimate, before you start comparing vehicles. If the number is
still moving, round up to the next size rather than the one below it. A few empty seats cost far
less than the stress of a bus that's too small on travel day.

## Match the Vehicle to the Trip, Not Just the Group

A 20-passenger group heading to the airport with luggage needs different storage than the same
group going to a game with just backpacks. Multi-day trips, equipment-heavy sports teams, and
airport transfers all need extra bay space, so it's worth sizing up or choosing a coach with
undercarriage storage even if the seat count alone would fit a smaller vehicle.

## Know the General Size Ranges

- **Sprinter vans and 15-passenger minibuses** — small groups, airport runs, and short local trips.
- **20 to 28-passenger minibuses** — mid-size school, church, or corporate groups.
- **30 to 40-passenger mini coaches** — larger teams and multi-stop itineraries with more storage.
- **50 to 56-passenger motorcoaches** — full-size groups, multi-day tours, and long-distance trips.

## Ask About Amenities for Longer Trips

For anything beyond a short transfer, ask what's onboard. Restrooms, reclining seats, and luggage
bays matter a lot more on a three-hour ride than a twenty-minute one, and not every vehicle size
comes standard with all of them.
